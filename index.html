IQCR: Inspection and Quality Control Robots

Can a six-axis inspection robot actually reach where it needs to?

IQCR proposes putting a camera, depth sensor, and laser probe on one robot arm instead of running parts between separate inspection stations. Before any of that sensor fusion gets built, the arm has to physically reach the poses the idea requires. This page runs the same ABB IRB 120 kinematics and the same inverse-kinematics solver as the paper, live in your browser, in 3D.

02. Does the solver actually work?

Convergence, live vs. the paper's MATLAB run

The paper ran this exact damped-least-squares solver (λ = 10⁻³, numerical Jacobian, tolerance 10⁻⁹) on 8 random poses: 7 converged to sub-micron accuracy in under 30 iterations, and 1 got stuck 32.7 mm off target after 1500 tries. That's a real failure mode of trial-and-error solvers, kept in the paper rather than hidden. Generate and solve a few targets above; each run lands in the chart and table below.

The MATLAB Robotics-Toolbox cross-check below (rst_verification.m) already used random restarts (AllowRandomRestart) and still hit the same stuck pose — evidence that particular target sits somewhere the arm genuinely struggles to reach cleanly, not just a tuning artifact. This live solver didn't have that safety net; it now does. When an attempt stalls it jumps to a fresh random joint configuration and retries, up to 5 times, keeping the closest result. Across 30,000 randomized offline trials spanning the full starting-guess-error slider range (±7.5° to ±45°), that took the stall rate from as high as ~0.5% down to 0%, with zero previously-successful cases made worse. A result badge below will say how many restarts a run needed.
